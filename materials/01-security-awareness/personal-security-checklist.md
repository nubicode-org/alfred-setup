# Personal security checklist

Work through one section per sitting. Tick an item only when it is done on every device and account
it applies to.

## Accounts

- [ ] A password manager holds every password, and I know its recovery process.
- [ ] My email account has a unique password and two-factor authentication. (Whoever controls your
      email can reset everything else.)
- [ ] Two-factor authentication is on for GitHub, my bank and my cloud accounts, using an app or a
      security key rather than SMS where the service allows it.
- [ ] I have saved the recovery codes for each of those somewhere I can reach without the phone.
- [ ] No two important accounts share a password. (Check the password manager's reuse report.)

## Devices

- [ ] The laptop's disk is encrypted (FileVault on macOS, BitLocker or Device Encryption on Windows,
      LUKS on Linux) and I know where the recovery key is.
- [ ] Automatic updates are on for the operating system and the browser.
- [ ] The screen locks after a few minutes and needs a password or fingerprint to open.
- [ ] The phone has a passcode of at least six digits, and "find my device" is on.

## Backups

- [ ] My important files are backed up automatically, to somewhere other than this laptop.
- [ ] I have restored at least one file from that backup, to prove it works.
- [ ] At least one copy is offline or versioned, so ransomware cannot encrypt the backup too.

## Habits

- [ ] I check where a link goes before signing in through it, and sign in by typing the address when
      in doubt.
- [ ] I verify an unusual request (money, credentials, urgency) through a second channel I already
      trust, not the one the request came on.
- [ ] I know who to tell at work or school if I click something I should not have, and I would tell
      them straight away.
